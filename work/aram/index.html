Agentic AI · Workflow Architecture

ARAM

An AI operating workflow designed to turn ambiguous requests into bounded execution, evidence-backed verification, and explicit return states.

RoleSystem designer / builder
FocusAgent orchestration & reliability
StatusOngoing system

01 · Problem

AI work breaks when responsibility is fuzzy.

As workflows grow across models, tools, repositories, and external services, the hard problem becomes coordination: who owns a decision, what can execute, what must be verified, and what happens when the state is uncertain.

02 · System

A workflow with explicit boundaries.

Resolve→Route→Execute→Verify→Return

ARAM treats an AI task as a stateful workflow rather than a single prompt. The public case study intentionally describes the architecture at a high level and excludes private operational details.

03 · Key decisions

Design for uncertainty.

  • Separate authority from execution. The system should know who may decide before asking who can act.
  • Prefer explicit states. Unknown, conflict, blocked, and verified are different outcomes.
  • Require evidence for completion. A successful tool response is not automatically a completed task.
  • Keep handoffs inspectable. The next executor should receive scope, constraints, evidence, and a return contract.

04 · Verification

Completion requires readback.

The workflow distinguishes proposed changes, execution, provider acknowledgement, and verified final state. Where practical, changes are followed by a readback that checks the intended postcondition instead of trusting a success banner.

Public evidence policy

Only sanitized architecture and externally safe evidence are published here. Internal operating records remain outside this repository.

05 · What this demonstrates

Agent orchestration

Task decomposition, routing, handoffs, and bounded tool use.

AI reliability

Evidence, explicit uncertainty, postcondition checks, and recovery paths.

Workflow design

Turning informal work into repeatable operating contracts.

Systems thinking

Connecting product, engineering, operations, and governance concerns.
